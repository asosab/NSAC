To decide what to build I sent an 18-question survey to Sueleen Peña Medina, director of Hacienda Patiño. The answers came in today, and they organize the project better than I had it.

What hurts most is forage. When to plant gets settled with weather forecasts from the web; how much forage to keep in reserve, with spot assessments in a few places; when the pasture hits its best point for silage, by the field technician's trained eye. Soil moisture has no method at all: it's the technician's feel. If they had a new tool tomorrow, she'd ask it to fine-tune forage harvest and planting times, and to spare them the slowest measurements, like the pasture assessments. When I asked about the last planting that went wrong, the missing data point was that one: the pasture's peak harvest point, when it's ready for the animals at its best nutritional condition.

How do they decide today? On a field walk. The technician and the farmhand walk the property, judge by eye which paddocks need reseeding or clearing, and the cowboys with the cattle manager decide where to rotate the animals. For planting they check forecasts and soil moisture, with experience on top. If rain comes late or cuts off, they stand exposed to losing germination percentage. A fact that corrects my brief: they don't plant corn, that was Pairumani. The species they use come out of their own trials, assigned by each paddock's character.

What data do they have? Records on paper and Excel. A full soil analysis once or twice a year, and they promised me an anonymized sample for Monday. Two weeks ago they installed their own weather station. Satellite images so far, just as a perimeter reference.

Who would use the tool first? That question went unanswered; still to be clarified. What did come through clear is the channel: WhatsApp is what they use most. On the digital side they run livestock software, but only for animal data. On indicators, they don't handle Pairumani data, and estimating the soil trend between lab analyses would help: today that's done by hand. On education they're open to learning, though that call belongs to the education manager.

What this does to the app:

- The product is forage, not grain. The pasture planting window and the harvest sweet spot are the real hook, and the starting catalog is their own pasture mix: Brachiarias, Gatton Panic, Stylosanthes, Calopogonium, Desmodium, Glycine and Archer.
- If soil moisture today is guesswork, showing the root-zone moisture NASA POWER computes is already a leap; their brand-new station becomes the local source once wired in.
- Paper and Excel means the parcel record is the product: climate series, soil baseline and their own measurements together.
- Decisions are made together, in the field. The app doesn't replace the technician: it hands them the record before the walk.
- If the channel is WhatsApp, the PDF report is the natural output; the web app stays with the technician.

Two debts left: Monday's soil sample and finding out who would use it first.
